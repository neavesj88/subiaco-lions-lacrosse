# Club details worksheet

Everything the website will ask you for, in one place. Fill this in **before**
you start the setup wizard — it is much easier to gather it once at a committee
meeting than to hunt for it halfway through setting the site up.

Nothing here is compulsory. Anything you leave blank simply doesn't appear on
the site, and you can add it later.

---

## 1. Who you are

| Question | Your answer | Where it appears |
|---|---|---|
| Club name, first line | `[e.g. Riverside Rovers]` | Logo, page titles, every heading |
| Club name, second line | `[e.g. Netball Club]` | Smaller line under the logo |
| Short name used in sentences | `[e.g. Rovers]` | "Join the Rovers", "Welcome to the Rovers" |
| Your sport, lowercase | `[e.g. netball]` | Used mid-sentence: "junior netball programs" |
| Your sport, capitalised | `[e.g. Netball]` | Used in headings |
| Year the club was founded | `[e.g. 1954]` | About page, and Google's club listing |
| One-line description of the club | `[e.g. Riverside's home of netball since 1954.]` | Under the logo, and in search results |

> **On the sport words:** the site uses these to build sentences automatically.
> Get the capitalisation right — "netball" lowercase and "Netball" capitalised —
> or you will see odd headings like "Junior netball Programs".

## 3. How people reach you

| Question | Your answer |
|---|---|
| Club email address | `[e.g. secretary@yourclub.org.au]` |
| Facebook page address | `[full https:// address, or leave blank]` |
| Instagram username | `[without the @, or leave blank]` |

> **Use a club role address, not a personal one.** `secretary@yourclub.org.au`
> keeps working when the secretary changes; `jane.smith@gmail.com` does not, and
> it publishes a volunteer's personal email to the whole internet.

## 4. Your colours

| Question | Your answer |
|---|---|
| Main club colour | `[e.g. #7B1E32]` |
| Darker shade of it | `[e.g. #3A0C17]` |
| Accent colour | `[e.g. #C5942A]` |

> Colours are written as a `#` followed by six characters. If you only know
> them by name, your uniform supplier or your logo designer will have the
> codes. Failing that, any online "colour picker" will give you one.
>
> **The site will warn you if white text won't be readable on the colour you
> pick.** Take that warning seriously — a colour that looks fine on your laptop
> can be unreadable on a phone in sunlight at a ground, which is exactly where
> people use club websites.

## 7. Who will run the website

| Role | Name | Email | Access level |
|---|---|---|---|
| Main administrator | `[name]` | `[email]` | Super Admin |
| | `[name]` | `[email]` | `[Club Admin / Juniors / Content / Social Media]` |
| | `[name]` | `[email]` | `[...]` |

> Give people the **least** access they need. A volunteer who only posts match
> photos should be a Social Media Manager — that role sees news and photos and
> literally nothing else, so there is nothing for them to break.
>
> Have **at least two** Super Admins. One person leaving the club should never
> lock everyone out.
